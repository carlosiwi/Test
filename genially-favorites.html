Genially · SaaS B2B/B2C · Growth

Favorites — helping creators
reuse what works.

Genially's template library is one of its differential values — but nobody had ever looked closely at the UX of that flow. This project went from open-ended discovery to a shipped feature that lifted creations per active user from 2.2 to 2.8 in its first 22 days.

Role
Product Designer — end-to-end
Team
PM, engineers & data, cross-team stakeholders
Methods
Interviews, journey mapping, Crazy 8's, usability testing
Platform
Web app — in-product feature
2.2 → 2.8 creations per active user, first 22 days after launch
>70% task success on first attempt in usability testing
2× retention for template users — the insight behind the bet
50 users completed the in-product test in just 2 days

01 — Context

Genially is a no-code tool for creating interactive content, used by millions of creators. The brief was deliberately open: improve the creation experience, with creations per active user (then at 2.2) as the north-star metric.

Rather than jumping to solutions, I started by understanding where the creation flow was winning and losing users.

02 — Discovery

To get relevant data fast I combined several sources: conversations with stakeholders, quantitative product data, research previously collected by other teams, and 6 user interviews.

What the evidence said

  • 80% of users create from templates — and metrics and ratings improve when they do.
  • Users who start from templates show twice the retention of those who start from scratch.
  • A high percentage of users drop out at the second step of the creation flow.
  • The breadth of the template library is one of Genially's differential values — yet its UX had never been analysed in depth.

That made the focus obvious: understand the value templates bring to users and how they influence the creation process.

03 — Going deeper

To map the template flow end-to-end I:

  • Analysed the existing user flows and built a journey map of the creation process.
  • Watched recorded user sessions to see real behaviour, not reported behaviour.
  • Ran a competitive analysis and a heuristic evaluation of the flow.

04 — Ideation & hypothesis

With the insights on the table, I facilitated a brainstorming session with stakeholders using Crazy 8's. Out of the ideas generated, the team aligned on the one with the best effort-to-evidence ratio:

If we let users save useful templates as favorites, they will return to them and create more — lifting creations per active user.

05 — Design & validation

I designed the flow for marking templates as favorites and a dedicated Favorites section, then validated it before writing a line of production code.

Usability testing, in-product

We launched an open task test from within the tool: mark a template as a favorite, then find the Favorites section. In 2 days, 50 people completed it:

  • Over 70% succeeded on the first attempt.
  • 20% succeeded on the second.

06 — Launch & results

An A/B test wasn't viable — the experiment was complex and there were technical constraints around persisting favorites — so we made a deliberate call: launch and measure, with clear success metrics agreed upfront. The first 22 days of data showed movement across the board:

  • Direct goal: creations per active user grew from 2.2 to 2.8.
  • Engagement: users adopted favorites and returned to them to create.
  • Retention and UX signals moved in the right direction.

07 — What I took away

  • Evidence narrows scope fast. Six interviews plus existing data were enough to turn an open brief into a sharp hypothesis.
  • Test where the users already are. Running the usability test in-product got us 50 sessions in 2 days at near-zero cost.
  • A perfect experiment isn't always available. When the A/B test fell through, agreeing on metrics before launch kept the team honest about the outcome.
